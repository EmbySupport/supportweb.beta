    AMF

    AMF Encoder Parameters

    AMD GPUs encode through the Advanced Media Framework (AMF). These parameters apply to the AMF encoders of the AMF on D3D11 and AMF on Vulkan context types - see AMD for the context types, and Encoders for how the dialog works. The VAAPI (AMD) context type uses the VAAPI encoders, and Vulkan (AMD) the Vulkan encoders.

    H.264

    The h264_amf encoder for H.264, offered by the AMF on D3D11 and AMF on Vulkan context types.

    Encoding Preset - trades encoding speed against quality (preset, ffmpeg preset). It has little effect with the Constant QP rate control method. Default: Prefer Speed.

    • Prefer Speed - prioritizes encoding speed, for real-time transcoding where keeping up with playback matters most
    • Balanced - a middle ground between speed and quality, suitable for most general-purpose transcoding
    • Prefer Quality - prioritizes quality, better suited to offline conversions than to live playback

    Usage - tells the encoder which scenario it is working in, which sets its internal defaults for latency-related behaviour (ffmpeg usage). A broader hint than the preset. Default: Transcoding.

    • Transcoding - general-purpose transcoding. The recommended choice unless one of the more specific scenarios applies
    • High Quality - tuned for the best achievable quality, without the latency constraints of the low-latency choices
    • Low Latency - reduced encoding latency, less aggressively than Ultra Low Latency, for interactive or near-real-time streaming
    • Low Latency, High Quality - aims for low latency and high quality at the same time - a narrower, more demanding scenario than either alone
    • Ultra Low Latency - the lowest possible encoding latency, at a cost in compression efficiency
    • Webcam - tuned for live camera capture such as video conferencing, rather than for transcoding existing media

    Rate Control Method - how the encoder reaches the bitrate Emby requests (ffmpeg rc). With the two constrained Variable Bitrate methods, only the target bitrate is passed to the encoder; with the other choices, the requested bitrate is also passed as the maximum rate, with a buffer of twice the bitrate. Default: Auto (driver default).

    • Auto (driver default) - sets no method, so the encoder chooses based on Usage and Encoding Preset. The recommended starting point
    • Constant QP - encodes every frame with the same fixed quantization parameter, using the encoder's default QP values: predictable per-frame quality, unpredictable size. Enable VBAQ has no effect in this mode
    • Constant Bitrate (CBR) - holds the bitrate as close to constant as possible, regardless of scene complexity (CBR). Useful for strict bandwidth limits, at the cost of quality consistency across scenes
    • Variable Bitrate, Peak-Constrained - targets an average bitrate that varies with scene complexity, with a hard peak limit (VBR). A common general-purpose choice for streaming
    • Variable Bitrate, Latency-Constrained - like Peak-Constrained, but additionally constrained to limit encoding latency
    • Quality-Based Variable Bitrate - targets a perceptual quality level instead of a bitrate, and lets the bitrate follow it within the overall limits (constant quality)
    • High-Quality Variable Bitrate - a higher-quality variant of variable bitrate, at a higher processing cost
    • High-Quality Constant Bitrate - a higher-quality variant of constant bitrate, at a higher processing cost

    Enable VBAQ - adaptive quantization: redistributes bits from complex areas of the picture to flat, low-detail areas, where blocking is most visible (ffmpeg vbaq). It has no effect with the Constant QP rate control method. Default: off.

    Low Latency Mode - requests low-latency behaviour inside the encoder (ffmpeg latency). Independent of the low-latency Usage choices, and can be combined with any of them. Default: off.

    Encoding Profile Limit - the highest H.264 profile the encoder may use; empty (the default) means no limit - see Encoding Profile Limit. Offers those of Constrained Baseline Profile, Main Profile and High Profile that the driver reports.

    HEVC

    Encoding Preset - trades encoding speed against quality (ffmpeg preset). It has little effect with the Constant QP rate control method. Default: Prefer Speed.

    • Prefer Speed - prioritizes encoding speed, for real-time transcoding where keeping up with playback matters most
    • Balanced - a middle ground between speed and quality, suitable for most general-purpose transcoding

    Tier - the HEVC tier, which sets the maximum bitrate allowed at the encoding level in use (ffmpeg profile_tier). Default: Main.

    • Main - the standard tier, sufficient for the vast majority of content and bitrates

    Encoding Profile Limit - the highest HEVC profile the encoder may use; empty (the default) means no limit - see Encoding Profile Limit. Offers those of Main Profile and Main 10 Profile that the driver reports. The encoder's bit depth follows the output, 8 or 10 bit.

    AV1

    Encoding Preset - trades encoding speed against quality (ffmpeg preset). It has little effect with the Constant QP rate control method. Default: Prefer Speed.

    • Balanced - a middle ground between speed and quality, suitable for most general-purpose transcoding
    • Prefer Quality - prioritizes quality, better suited to offline conversions than to live playback
    • Highest Quality - the highest-quality preset, above Prefer Quality, at the highest processing cost

    Usage - tells the encoder which scenario it is working in, which sets its internal defaults for latency-related behaviour (ffmpeg usage). A broader hint than the preset. Default: Transcoding.

With the two constrained Variable Bitrate methods, only the target bitrate is passed to the encoder; with the other choices, the requested bitrate is also passed as the maximum rate, with a buffer of twice the bitrate.     • Auto (driver default) - sets no method, so the encoder chooses based on Usage and Encoding Preset. The recommended starting point

    The encoder's bit depth follows the output, 8 or 10 bit. AV1 encoders have no Encoding Profile Limit.
